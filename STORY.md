# Shardfall — The Story

> *Every time they fail, they remember a little more of a future they have already lived.*

Twenty years ago the crystal that lit a kingdom shattered in a single night. A disgraced knight, an exiled prodigy and a shrine healer set out to reforge it, carrying the one shard that can turn back time. Every chapter of their journey is a road they may have to walk many times: each fall returns them to the last moment time was fixed, and each fall leaves them with a **Memory**, a piece of a future they have already lived.

Underneath the journey is a mystery the game tells from five sides: **what really happened the night the light broke.**

## Themes

- **Comfort paid for by someone else.** The kingdom's endless daylight was bought with one child at a time, and nobody wanted to know.
- **Holding on versus letting go.** Seren rewinds the world rather than lose her friends. A future Aldric would kill the girl he loves rather than watch her be taken. The archbishop sacrificed a child rather than face the dark. All three are acts of love, and all three are traps.
- **Looking back.** The first words Aldric ever hears from his hero are *"Don't look back."* The whole game, and its central mechanic, argues the opposite: memory is how you do better.

---

## The truth of the world

*(What the player learns over the whole game. The characters believe something else at the start.)*

**The Lumen** was built three hundred years ago by the first Order of Dawn, to end the **Long Dusk**: the natural, harsh cycle of dark seasons the land had always had. It is not a god. It is a vessel that **holds daylight**, stores it and releases it steadily, so Valemont never knew a hard winter again.

A vessel needs a **Heart**. Whenever a Heart dies, about once a decade, the Order chooses a child, the "Saint of Dawn", who is **Ascended** into the crystal and lives inside it, asleep, keeping the light turning until they die and the next child is chosen. Thirty-one children in three hundred years. The Order tells the faithful that the Saints ascend to the gods.

**The Shardfall, first cause.** Twenty years ago the Saint chosen was a nine-year-old girl named **Ilse**. Her brother **Caius**, a young knight-captain of Valemont, found out what the Ascension really was on the night it was performed. He broke into the sanctum and struck the crystal to cut her out. A vessel with a living Heart inside cannot be opened from outside. The Lumen shattered.

- The capital burned under a rain of crystal.
- **Ilse died** before her brother could get back to her.
- On his way out of the fire, Caius carried a **boy** out of the rubble. The boy was Aldric. Caius's armour was streaked with crystal.
- The priest who had performed the Ascension, **Maelis**, survived in the sanctum doorway.
- A splinter from the Lumen's core, the part that had been touching the Heart, struck a small girl in the street. **Lyra.**

**What came after.**
- **The Dimming:** with nothing holding the light, the land is sliding back into the Long Dusk. The Order preaches it as the end of the world. It is only the world as it was before the Lumen: harder, darker, survivable.
- **The Keystone:** the splinter in Lyra's heart is the only piece that ever touched a Heart. The Lumen cannot be reforged without it, and **the Keystone only works if its bearer walks into the Lumen willingly.**
- **The Wardens:** the Order spent twenty years trying to make a new Keystone by fusing people with great shards. Every attempt produced a **Warden**, a crystal guardian with a person trapped inside.
- **Caius** fled to the empire of Kaldra, rose as a general, took the throne, and has spent twenty years hunting shards and throwing them into the Abyss, so that no child is ever put inside the light again. The Order made him the continent's villain.
- **Maelis** became Archbishop. He believes, sincerely, that the Long Dusk will kill millions. He has buried children in hard winters. He would put another child in the light tomorrow, and he hates himself for it.

## The rules of time

| | |
|---|---|
| **The Hourglass** | A shard that fell on the village of Emberfall on the night of the Shardfall. Every shard holds a little of the daylight the Lumen stored; this one can hold a *moment*. It is kept in Emberfall's shrine, the Anchorlight. |
| **Anchors** | Its keeper can fix a moment (an Anchor) and, when everything falls apart, return everyone who has touched the Hourglass to that moment. The Hourglass always holds two: **the last Anchor** and **the first**, the morning it woke. |
| **Memories** | Those who touched it wake from a rewind with fragments of what happened. The **keeper** wakes with everything, and the Memories the others receive pass through her, so she can shape them. |
| **The cost** | Rewinding costs the keeper nothing she can see. Falling *out* of time costs a great deal: anyone carried outside the Anchors slowly turns to crystal. |

**How this plays:** each chapter is a roguelite journey toward one goal. Fail, and you return to the chapter's Anchor with a new Memory that gives a real advantage next time. Reach the goal, and Seren sets a new Anchor: the chapter's events become permanent and the story moves on.

Memory types: **Foresight** (battle knowledge), **Pathfinding** (routes and secrets), **People** (change someone's fate), **Techniques** (new cards), **Fragments** (pieces of a hidden scene).

**The Shardfall mosaic.** Five Fragments, found across the game, each replay the Prologue's night from someone else's eyes: *the boy, the priest, the brother, the girl in the light, the girl in the street.* Together they reveal the first cause. Completing the mosaic unlocks the true ending.

---

## Why this journey is different

This is the **four hundred and twelfth** time the party has made this journey. The previous 411 all ended the same way: Lyra, believing the Order, walked into the Lumen to end the Dimming, and Seren went back to the first morning to try again.

This time there is someone new on the road: **the Ashen Knight**, the Aldric of loop 411. At the end of that loop he tried to stop Seren rewinding, they struggled, and the Hourglass split in two. Seren's half pulled her back to the first morning as always. His half threw him out of time, a year before the journey begins. He knew Caius was right, so he went to Kaldra and became his sword.

Every difference between this journey and the 411 before it traces back to him. It is the first loop where anyone learns the truth in time.

# Act I — The Pilgrimage

## Prologue — The Night the Light Broke

*Twenty years ago.*

A boy wakes under a fallen roof in a city where light is falling out of the sky. The great crystal above the capital is breaking, not cracking but coming apart, as if something inside it had been struck. Through the smoke comes a knight, armour streaked with crystal, carrying a small body wrapped in a cloak. He sets the body down, lifts the boy instead of it, and carries him through the fire and out of the gates.

*"Don't look back."*

The boy looks back anyway. On the steps of the burning cathedral a priest is kneeling, very still. In the street a little girl clutches her chest, where something bright has just gone in.

When the boy turns around, the knight has gone back into the fire.

**Plays:** a short walk out of the burning city as the child. It can't be failed. It sets up every image the game will return to.

**What the player doesn't notice yet:** the knight put down a body to carry the boy, and then went back for it.

---

## Chapter 1 — Emberfall

**Goal:** get Seren and the Anchorlight's relic out of Emberfall alive.

Aldric is escorting an Order supply caravan to Emberfall, a shrine village at the edge of the Dimming. In the shrine library he finds a runaway mage using a false name, Lyra. At the shrine, the keeper, Seren, asks him to help her carry the relic to the cellar before the weather turns. It's heavier than it looks. He doesn't think about it again.

At dusk Kaldra's soldiers come over the ridge, led by the Ashen Knight. Aldric faces him on the shrine steps and loses in three strokes.

**He wakes at dawn.** The same bed, the same smell of bread, the same caravan outside. Seren is in the doorway with the relic in her hands, glowing like a sunrise.

*"You're awake,"* she says. *"Good. We don't have long."*

**Memories:** *The soldiers cross the north bridge at dusk* (Pathfinding: collapse it) · *The Ashen Knight always guards his left* (Foresight) · *The miller's daughter hides in the well* (People: save her, and the village's gratitude outfits the party).

**Twist:** on the second attempt the party leaves by a different road, and **the Ashen Knight is already on it.** He remembers too.

**Ending:** cornered on the hill, the Knight raises his blade over Lyra, and stops, for one breath, as if the motion were familiar. It is enough to escape. Seren sets the first Anchor on the hill above the burning village.

**Why it matters later:** Seren asked Aldric to carry the relic in every one of the 412 loops, so that he would touch it and come back with them.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Road of Lanterns

**Goal:** bring the Hourglass to Archbishop Maelis in the holy city of Luminar.

A long road of pilgrim shrines and frightened villages, where the harvest came in grey and the winter came early. The party learns to be a party. Lyra is merciless about Aldric's knightly habits. Aldric carries everyone's pack without being asked. Seren always knows which inn has a room and which bridge is out.

In Luminar, Maelis receives them like family. He knows grief when he sees it and speaks to Aldric about Elowen with real tenderness. He explains the Order's hope: gather the great shards, reforge the Lumen, bring back the light. For the first time in three years Aldric has a cause.

**Warden:** the Lantern Warden, a suit of pilgrim's armour full of guttering light, guarding the first great shard. When it breaks, a pilgrim's token falls out of the crystal with a girl's name scratched on it. Nobody knows what it means yet.

**Memories:** *The lamplighter of Hollin falls from his tower at dawn* (People: catch him, and he shows you the old shrine road) · *The Warden's flame dies if its lantern breaks first* (Foresight) · **Fragment 1, the priest:** *a young Maelis in white robes, walking a small girl by the hand down a corridor of light. She asks if it will be dark inside. He says no.*

**Unease:** Seren never told Maelis what the Hourglass does. He talks about it as if he'd read a report.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Mirror Lake

**Goal:** recover the great shard beneath the Mirror Lake.

A lake so still it reflects a sky that isn't there: stars at noon, a sunrise that never finishes. The Warden that rises from it has the face Aldric sees every night.

**Princess Elowen.** Crystal where her skin should be, her voice like struck glass. She knows him. She isn't fighting to kill him; the crystal is fighting through her.

The Warden can't be beaten by force; straight fights fail every time. The answer is a **People** Memory from an earlier attempt: *"You never finished the song."* Aldric sings the song she used to hum on the palace walls. Elowen stops fighting, and lets the crystal break.

**Reveal:** Wardens are people. Maelis, grieving with them, tells the party Kaldra's sorcerers did this to her during the war. Aldric believes him because he needs someone to blame, and the next chapter runs on that anger.

**What the player may notice:** Seren sang the second verse with him. She shouldn't know it.

---

# Act II — The War

## Chapter 4 — The Iron March

**Goal:** infiltrate the imperial fortress of Varrow and take Kaldra's shards.

A grey country of foundries and conscripts, where the Order's sun-sign is scratched off doorframes. This chapter plays differently: **the Ashen Knight is rewinding against you.** Routes that were open close on the next attempt, garrisons move to where you went last time, contacts are arrested before you reach them. It's a chess match against someone who can also take back moves.

Beneath Varrow there's no hoard of shards. There's a chasm, and the Emperor standing at its edge, dropping a great shard into the dark. **Caius** is older than his portraits, and very tired.

*"Your Archbishop needs one more thing to finish what he started. When you find out what it is, remember I tried."*

The Ashen Knight steps out of the dark beside him, and the duel on the chasm bridge begins.

**Midpoint twist:** Aldric's blade splits the Knight's helm. Underneath is **his own face**, older, half grown over with crystal. The Knight doesn't explain himself. He looks past Aldric to Lyra, and moves.

*"I'm sorry. It's kinder now."*

Seren rewinds.

On the attempt that succeeds, the party escapes Varrow with the Emperor's help, and Order assassins, sent to silence Caius, find him first. He dies on the bridge with Aldric holding the wound. His last words are to someone who isn't there.

*"I went back for you. I'm sorry I stopped for the boy."*

Aldric doesn't understand. He will.

**Fragment 2, the brother:** *a young knight kicking in the doors of a sanctum, a crystal the size of a cathedral, a small shape asleep inside it.*

---

## Chapter 5 — The Veiled Academy

**Goal:** reach the Academy's sealed archive and learn why the Ashen Knight wants Lyra dead.

Lyra's chapter. The Academy was the Order's all along. It takes in gifted children, and it has one child it never stopped watching.

This is where the Memories turn on the player. A Memory from an earlier attempt says *"The archive's east door is unguarded at midnight."* It isn't. More prove wrong: a guide who sells you out, a "safe" road that isn't. **Some Memories are false.** From here on, Memories whose origin is uncertain are marked with a hairline crack, and the player has to decide which to trust.

The party is sure the lies come from the Ashen Knight. He shares the Hourglass, after all.

In the archive: Lyra's file, begun the morning after the Shardfall. A splinter from the Lumen's core in a child's heart. **The Keystone.** The Lumen can't be reforged without it, and the Keystone only works if its bearer walks into the light **willingly**. Twenty years of reports on her temper, her friendships, her fear of the dark. A note in Maelis's hand: *"She must come to it herself. Give her a reason."*

And the other files: every Warden, with a name. The pilgrim girl from the Lantern Warden's token. Elowen, taken from the palace during the invasion and delivered to the Order, not to Kaldra.

Lyra reads her own file standing up and doesn't sit down for a long time. That night she writes a letter to a mother she barely remembers, and burns it.

*"Twenty years. They were waiting for me to be sad enough."*

**Fragment 3, the girl in the street:** *a child in a burning street, watching a light fall toward her. She doesn't run. She doesn't know to.*

---

## Chapter 6 — Four Hundred and Twelve

**Goal:** return to Emberfall and mend the Hourglass before it breaks.

The Hourglass is failing. Each rewind costs a little more: a street missing when they come back, an innkeeper who doesn't remember them. Seren says only the Anchorlight can mend it, and Emberfall is ash; they walk through the village they fled in Chapter 1.

At the shrine, mending the Hourglass means opening it, and the Hourglass remembers.

**Twist:** this is not the first journey. It is the **four hundred and twelfth**.

Seren tells them, without being asked, because she's tired. She has remembered every loop. In all of them, Lyra walked into the light at the end, and Seren went back to the first morning, because *this time* might be the one where she doesn't. She has shaped what the others remember: kept the worst away, and when she had to, **given them false Memories**, the archive door, the guide, to keep them off roads that broke them in other loops. The song is Elowen's; she learned it in a loop where they reached the lake in time.

**Aldric:** *"How many times have I died?"*
**Seren:** *"You don't want the number."*
**Aldric:** *"I'm asking."*
**Seren:** *"A hundred and seventy. I stopped counting the ones that were my fault."*

She tells them the rest. The Hourglass split at the end of loop 411. The other half is inside the Ashen Knight's armour. Without it, the Hourglass can't be mended.

**Changes:** from now on the attempt counter reads **Loop 412**, and the chapter's rewinds count up from there. The party walks a whole road in silence. At the next camp Lyra sits down next to Seren without a word. Aldric doesn't forgive her yet. He carries her pack anyway.

**Fragment:** *Seren, alone at the shrine on the morning she first rewound, not yet knowing what she has started.*

---

# Act III — The Light

## Chapter 7 — The Ashen Road

**Goal:** find the Ashen Knight and take back the Hourglass's other half.

The party hunts the man who has been hunting them. He is dying. The crystal has reached his throat, and every rewind outside the Anchors spreads it further. They find him in the ruins of the old capital, where the Shardfall began, sitting on the steps of the burnt cathedral.

He doesn't fight. He talks. He has had 411 loops to learn what the Order buried, and one year in Kaldra's archives to prove it.

He tells them the first cause. Caius, Ilse, the sanctum, the struck crystal, the rain of light. And the knight in the rubble.

**Twist:** the knight who carried Aldric out of the fire was **Caius**. Aldric's hero, the man whose example he has tried to live up to for twenty years, is the tyrant he let die on the bridge. *"I went back for you. I'm sorry I stopped for the boy."* Caius had put his sister down to save a stranger's child, and she died before he got back to her.

The Knight's plan hasn't changed: Lyra must die before she can choose. *"She always chooses it. I watched her choose it four hundred times."* The party's plan is to prove the Order wrong before the Reforging. They need his half of the Hourglass; he needs them not to fail. An alliance of the worst kind.

**He joins as a guest**: two Aldrics in one party, younger and older. They argue about everything except how to fight.

**Fragment 4, the girl in the light:** *Ilse, asleep inside the crystal, feels something strike it, and opens her eyes.*

---

## Chapter 8 — The Voice in the Light

**Goal:** reach the Cathedral of Dawn before the Reforging.

Luminar is holding the ceremony of the century. Every great shard is in the Cathedral; the faithful fill the streets with candles; the Order has declared the Dimming's end. The party has proof: Lyra's file, the Wardens' names, the ledger of three hundred years of Saints. And, from the Knight, the Order's own founding records: **the Long Dusk is not the end of the world.** It's what the world was like before the Lumen. Harder winters. Shorter days. People lived.

Maelis doesn't deny any of it.

*"I held her hand all the way to the light. She asked me if it would be dark inside. I said no. Every morning for twenty years I have woken up knowing I lied to a child. And every winter, I have buried children the light would have saved. If you want me to tell you I was wrong, I can't."*

**Twist:** Maelis doesn't need Lyra to walk in willingly any more. When the party breaks the ceremony, he steps into the half-reforged Lumen **himself**. He isn't a Keystone. The crystal takes him anyway, and everything it has ever taken: the great shards, the Wardens' remnants, three hundred years of stored light.

**Fragment 5, the boy:** *the Prologue again, and this time the camera follows the knight back into the fire, to the small body he put down.* The mosaic is complete.

---

## Chapter 9 — The Hollow of Dawn

**Goal:** end it.

Inside the Lumen: a cathedral of held light, where the three centuries of stored days hang like stained glass. Morning markets, summer fields, a hundred festivals, all perfect and all bought. At its centre, what became of Maelis: **the Crystal Wyrm**, a Warden made of every Warden, speaking with the Archbishop's patience.

The fight is the hardest in the game. In its last exchange the Wyrm's strike comes for young Aldric, and the Ashen Knight takes it. The crystal finishes him. He presses the other half of the Hourglass into his younger self's hand.

*"I stopped trusting them. Don't."*

The Wyrm falls. The Lumen stands open and humming, full of light and with no Heart. And there is still the Keystone.

**The choice:**
- **Reforge.** Lyra walks in, as she always has. The Dimming ends. The credits roll over a cathedral of light, and then over Seren's hands on the Hourglass, and a caravan arriving at Emberfall. **New Game+** is canonically loop 413: every Memory unlocked, and every chapter a little different because Seren knows more.
- **Release.** *Only possible with the Shardfall mosaic complete.* Knowing the whole truth, Lyra uses the Keystone the other way: not to lock a Heart inside the light, but to open the vessel for good.

---

## Finale — Held Light

Lyra turns the Keystone.

Three hundred years of stored daylight leave the Lumen at once, and for one long morning the whole continent is lit brighter than it has ever been: the last dawn the crystal will ever give. The Lumen goes clear, then dark, then still.

The Keystone was the only thing keeping Lyra's heart beating; the Shardfall broke it when she was four. She sits down on the cathedral floor and starts, out of habit, to count.

Seren fits the two halves of the Hourglass together. Whole, it can hold one more moment. She doesn't set an Anchor. She uses it to return one thing, **Lyra's heart**, to the morning before the Shardfall, before the splinter. The Hourglass empties and crumbles in her hands. Four hundred and twelve loops, and she spends the last of it on one heartbeat.

The cathedral is coming down. Aldric lifts Lyra, as a knight once lifted him, and carries her out.

At the door Seren tells him not to look back.

He does. He looks at all of it, the empty crystal, the Knight's armour on the floor, the stained-glass days going dark, and he carries it with him out into the ordinary light.

---

## Epilogue — The First Winter

*One year later.*

The first real winter Valemont has had in three hundred years. It's hard, and short-houred, and people get through it, because they help each other and because Kaldra, under a new council, sends grain north.

Every town has a lantern festival now. In Luminar, the plaque that said *"Saint of Dawn, Ascended"* has been replaced with thirty-one names.

Aldric visits a grave on the chasm bridge at Varrow and leaves a crystal-streaked pauldron on it. Lyra teaches a class of frightened children how to make fire behave. Seren rebuilds the Anchorlight in Emberfall without a relic in it. It's just a shrine now, for remembering.

The last scene is the three of them on the shrine steps at dusk, as the lanterns come on. Lyra, lying back on the stone, counting under her breath, stops, and laughs.

*"What?"* Aldric asks.

*"Nothing. It's just one."*

## Writing notes

- **Nobody explains themselves at the moment of a twist.** The Ashen Knight is unmasked and says one line. Caius's truth arrives three chapters after his death. Let the player do the arithmetic.
- **No villain speeches.** Maelis gets one paragraph, and it's a confession, not a threat.
- **Humour comes from character, never from winking at the audience.** Lyra is funny because she's defended; Pom is funny because he's slightly worried about the potion.
- **Every sacrifice is a choice made by the person who pays for it.** Ilse didn't choose, which is the original sin of the story; the ending only works because Lyra, Seren and the Knight do.
